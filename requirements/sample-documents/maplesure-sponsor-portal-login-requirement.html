1. Business objective

Give every plan sponsor user a personal, secure login page for SponsorConnect — so that each sponsor administrator signs in as themselves, MapleSure can attribute every claim submission to a named person, and the online disability claim submission journey can go live on a trusted front door.

Plan sponsors are the employer organisations that sponsor coverage for their members. Today a sponsor's HR team shares one organisation-level password to reach SponsorConnect, which means MapleSure cannot say who uploaded a claim form, who viewed a member's status, or when a departed employee's access was actually removed.

2. Background and current state

  • SponsorConnect is an existing application. It currently authenticates with a single credential per sponsor organisation, issued by the onboarding team and passed around the sponsor's HR staff by email.
  • Individual user records already exist in the sponsor administration back office (name, work email, sponsor organisation, role), but nothing in the portal uses them to sign a person in.
  • The online disability claim submission programme (EPIC-S7-001) needs a named submitter on every claim. Its compliance review will not approve go-live while the portal cannot identify the individual who submitted a claim on a member's behalf.
  • Support volume for "we lost the sponsor password" is the second most common sponsor contact, and every reset re-issues one shared credential to the whole organisation.

3. Requirements

  1. Personal login page. A sponsor user signs in on a single, clearly branded MapleSure login page with their work email address and a personal password; the shared organisation credential is retired at cut-over and no longer accepted.
  2. Sponsor context on sign-in. A successful sign-in establishes the user's sponsor organisation and portal role (Sponsor Administrator or HR User) from the sponsor administration record, and the portal header shows the signed-in person's name and their organisation's plan number.
  3. Multi-sponsor users. A user who is authorised for more than one sponsor organisation (a benefits consultant, for example) is shown a choice of organisation immediately after sign-in and acts within only the one chosen for that session.
  4. Generic failure message. A failed sign-in shows one generic message — it must not reveal whether the email address is known, or whether the password was wrong.
  5. Temporary lockout. After five consecutive failed attempts for the same email address, sign-in for that address is locked for fifteen minutes; the page says so without confirming the address exists, and a Sponsor Administrator can see the lockout in their user list.
  6. Self-serve password reset. A "Forgotten your password?" link sends a single-use reset link to the user's registered work email address, valid for thirty minutes; the page's response is the same whether or not the address is registered.
  7. Session timeout. A signed-in session expires after twenty minutes of inactivity, with a warning shown two minutes before, and the user is returned to the login page with an explanation rather than an error.
  8. Access removal. When a Sponsor Administrator deactivates a user in the sponsor administration record, that user cannot sign in from that moment, and any active session for them ends within five minutes.
  9. Audit log. Every sign-in attempt, lockout, password reset request and session expiry is recorded with the email address, sponsor organisation, timestamp, outcome and originating network address — and never the password or the reset link.
  10. Accessibility. The login and organisation-choice pages are fully keyboard-accessible, label every field, announce errors to assistive technology, and meet WCAG AA contrast on the MapleSure palette.
  11. Credential storage. Passwords are never stored or logged in plain text; the credential store holds salted hashes only, and reset links are stored only as a hash of the token.

4. Out of scope

  • Multi-factor authentication and federation with sponsor employers' own identity providers (planned as a following phase once individual accounts exist).
  • Sponsor user creation, role assignment and deactivation screens — these already exist in the sponsor administration back office; this request only makes the portal honour them.
  • Member-facing sign-in, which is delivered separately under REQ-2026-121.
  • Any change to the claim submission forms themselves.

5. Success measures

  • Every claim submitted through SponsorConnect after cut-over carries the named sponsor user who submitted it.
  • The shared organisation credential is refused on every sponsor account from the cut-over date.
  • A user with valid personal credentials reaches their sponsor's portal home in one attempt; a multi-sponsor user reaches the organisation choice in one attempt.
  • Invalid attempts and reset requests never disclose whether an email address is registered.
  • "Lost sponsor password" contacts fall by at least half within one quarter of cut-over.
  • Zero passwords or reset tokens in any log or audit record.

6. Notes for delivery

This is an enhancement to a live application, not a new one: reuse the existing sponsor administration user records as the source of truth for identity and role, and keep the change to the login page, the organisation-choice step, the session handling and the audit log. The existing shared-credential path stays behind a feature switch until cut-over so the two can run side by side during sponsor migration. Synthetic sponsor and user data only.
